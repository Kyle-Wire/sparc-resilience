What cools

What each change does as you add more of it, where extra effort stops paying off, and how far the cooling spreads.

Diminishing returns

Every place was given growing amounts of each change, and the full model worked out the cooling each time. The curves show the citywide average cooling; the shaded band is the likely range. The bar under each title shows how the cooling builds up place by place. See the Diminishing returns theme on the map.

The numbers behind these curves, change by change, are in Model details › Diminishing returns by change.

How far does a change reach?

How far from the spot each change still affects the air temperature; 60 m, the shortest distance measured, means it acts on the spot and its neighbours.

Adaptation scenarios

Each bar is how much a scenario changes the afternoon air temperature, averaged over the whole city; the thin line spans most places (the middle 80%). Hollow bars are scenarios where more than 1 in 5 changed places go beyond anything measured in the city today, so treat them as rough.

      Reach and direction by map layer

      Reach: how far each map layer still affects the air temperature around it, measured from how temperatures and the layer vary together with distance. The model looks at each layer averaged over neighbourhoods of half, one and two times these distances. 60 m is the shortest distance measured (a place and its neighbours), so a layer at 60 m acts locally.

      Direction (anisotropy): the ratio of the short to the long axis of the correlation ellipse (1 = the same in every direction) and the long axis's bearing (compass degrees). "Reliable" means a bootstrap (re-fitting on resampled data) confirmed it.

      Do the model's effects survive a causal check?

      An independent estimate from double machine learning with spatial cross-fitting, split into the effect of a cell's own cover and its neighbours' cover. The audit compares like with like: the model's neighbourhood-adoption slope against the causal own + neighbour effect, and the model's own-cell slope against the causal own-cell effect. E-value and robustness value say how strong a hidden confounder would need to be to explain the estimate away.
