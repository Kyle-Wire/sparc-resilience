How far does a change reach?

The area of influence of each variable: the distance over which its neighbourhood still explains air temperature, from a distributed-lag fit of the correlogram. The models use neighbourhood averages at half, one and two times these ranges. 60 m is the floor (the cell and its neighbours), so a 60 m variable acts locally.

Influence range by variable

Direction (anisotropy)

Ratio of the short to the long axis of the correlation ellipse (1 = the same in every direction) and the long axis's bearing. "Reliable" means a bootstrap confirmed it.

Where does more cooling still pay off?

Every cell's neighbourhood was given increasing doses of each intervention and re-predicted through the full model. The curves show the city-wide mean cooling; the band is a 95% interval from a jackknife over the {{N_FOLDS}} fold models. Per cell, a saturating curve A·(1 − e−dose/d) was fitted against the dose the neighbourhood actually received, which gives the maximum cooling, the dose that reaches 90% of it, and today's marginal benefit. See the Saturation theme on the map.

Adaptation scenarios

Each scenario edits the land cover, updates NDVI through its fitted dependence on canopy and impervious cover, rebuilds the neighbourhood features and re-predicts through the {{N_FOLDS}} fold models. Bars show the mean change in air temperature; whiskers span the 10th to 90th percentile across cells. Scenarios that push many cells outside the observed range of conditions are flagged. The orange marks are an independent check: the causal own + neighbour effect from the causal check below, times the average change each scenario applies, with its 95% band. It is a straight-line extrapolation, so expect it to drift from the model at large doses where the response saturates.

Do the model's effects survive a causal check?

An independent estimate from double machine learning with spatial cross-fitting, split into the effect of a cell's own cover and its neighbours' cover. The audit compares like with like: the model's neighbourhood-adoption slope against the causal own + neighbour effect, and the model's own-cell slope against the causal own-cell effect. E-value and robustness value say how strong a hidden confounder would need to be to explain the estimate away.

Methods & validation

What a reviewer would ask before trusting the numbers: what is in the data, which weather drove the physics, whether the model beats standard tools on the same held-out areas, whether it finds effects for layers that cannot matter, whether it recovers effects planted on this city's own layout, whether its conclusions survive other reasonable analysis choices, how it compares with published cooling rates, and whether a run can be reproduced.
