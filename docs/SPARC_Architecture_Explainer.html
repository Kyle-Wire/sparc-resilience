Section 2

Stage 0 — The Spatial Geometry Engine

Before any model trains, SPARC measures the intrinsic geometry of the data. Every downstream decision about block size, bandwidth, and spatial attention is grounded here.

Moran's I Spatial Autocorrelation
Why are nearby things similar?

For each variable at multiple distance lags, SPARC computes Moran's I — a measure of spatial clustering. An FFT-accelerated implementation makes this tractable for large grids.

Moran's I
$$I = \frac{N}{\sum_{ij} w_{ij}} \cdot \frac{\sum_{ij} w_{ij}(x_i - \bar{x})(x_j - \bar{x})}{\sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2}$$

$w_{ij}$ = spatial weight between location $i$ and $j$. Values near +1 = clustered, near -1 = dispersed.

Bayesian Matérn Fitting via NUTS
What is the effective range?

The correlogram shape is fit to a Matérn covariance function using full Bayesian inference (NUTS sampler). This gives a posterior over the length scale κ and smoothness ν — not just a point estimate.

Matérn Covariance
$$k(d) = \sigma^2 \cdot \frac{2^{1-\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu)} \left(\sqrt{2\nu}\frac{d}{\kappa}\right)^\nu K_\nu\!\left(\sqrt{2\nu}\frac{d}{\kappa}\right)$$

$K_\nu$ = modified Bessel function. $\kappa$ = length scale. $\nu$ = smoothness. SPARC fits both with NUTS.

KernelField Output
The wiring harness for every downstream model

Stage 0 doesn't just print numbers. It outputs a KernelField object — a per-variable record of spatial geometry that auto-wires into every downstream component:

  • Anisotropy parameters (κ_x, κ_y, θ) — direction matters; heat doesn't diffuse the same way in every direction
  • Bandwidth-to-outcome distance — tells Stage 2 surrogates how wide their local windows should be
  • V×V effective-range matrix — cross-correlogram between all variable pairs
  • Spatial CV block size — prevents data leakage in fold splitting

Section 3

SPARCMetaLearner — The Neural Architecture

A two-part neural network: a SharedTrunk that encodes physical laws and transfers across cities, and a CityHead that specializes to local spatial statistics. Together they predict temperature anomalies with calibrated uncertainty.

SIREN — Sinusoidal Representation Network
Why sine activations instead of ReLU?

Normal neural networks use ReLU activations. SIREN uses $\sin(\omega_0 \cdot \mathbf{W}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b})$. The reason is mathematical: sine waves are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian.

SIREN Layer
$$\mathbf{h}_{i+1} = \sin\!\left(\omega_0 \cdot \mathbf{W}_i \mathbf{h}_i + \mathbf{b}_i\right)$$

$\omega_0 = 30$ (first layer), 1 otherwise. Because $\nabla^2 \sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}) = -\|\mathbf{k}\|^2 \sin(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x})$, the network can exactly represent PDE solutions.

This is why the physics encoder can blend harmonic solutions (boundary-dominated heat flow) with forcing-variable responses. It's not just a clever choice — it's physically principled.

SparseSpatialAttention
Attention over your neighborhood, not the whole city

Standard transformer attention is $O(N^2)$ — unusable for spatial grids. SPARC uses K-nearest neighbor attention so each point only attends to its local neighborhood.

KNN Multi-Head Attention
$$\text{Attn}(i) = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_k(i)} \frac{\exp(q_i^T k_j / \sqrt{d} - \lambda \cdot d_{ij})}{\sum_{j'} \exp(\cdot)} v_j$$

Distance scale $\lambda$ is learnable and initialized from the Stage 0 KernelField bandwidth. The geometry is physically grounded from day 1.

Section 4

The Physics Engine — PDE Operators

SPARC doesn't just fit data. It enforces that predictions must be consistent with the physical laws governing heat transfer and energy balance. These constraints are computed differentiably so they can be backpropagated through.

What this means in practice: A purely data-driven model could predict a temperature pattern that violates the laws of thermodynamics — e.g., heat flowing uphill, or energy appearing from nowhere. SPARC's PDE terms are added to the loss function as soft constraints that penalize physically impossible predictions during training. The network learns to be physically consistent, not just statistically fit.
Boundary Conditions
The edges matter

Real physical domains have boundaries. SPARC implements all three classical boundary condition types:

  • Neumann — no heat flux through domain edges (∂T/∂n = 0)
  • Dirichlet — fixed temperature at water bodies
  • Robin — mixed radiation balance at land-water interfaces
ProcessRateNet
Learning how land cover changes treatment response

A separate neural network maps land-cover proportions to spatially-varying treatment response rates α(s). Pre-trained on land-cover classification, then injected as a prior into the main PDE encoder.

Alpha spatial field
$$\alpha(s) = \text{ProcessRateNet}(\text{land\_cover}(s))$$

α modulates how strongly a given intervention (e.g., tree planting) reduces temperature at location s.

Section 5

Loss Functions — What the Network Optimizes

Training is guided by a joint loss that combines data fit, surrogate alignment, spatial consistency, and 10 physics terms. Each term has a weight, and those weights are optimized by CMA-ES.

Joint Loss
$$\mathcal{L}_\text{total} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{data}}_{\text{MSE + class}} + \lambda_s \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{surr}}_{\text{surrogate align}} + \lambda_n \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{nbr}}_{\text{neighborhood}} + \lambda_\sigma \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{smooth}}_{\text{smooth}} + \lambda_\text{PDE} \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{PDE}}_{\text{10 terms}} + \lambda_J \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{JEPA}}_{\text{latent align}}$$

All $\lambda$ weights are optimized by CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) — an evolutionary optimizer that tunes the balance across terms without manual grid search.

# PDE Term Activates at Epoch Formula Physical Meaning
1 Heat Diffusion 1 (always on) $\alpha \nabla^2 T - S \approx 0$ Laplacian of temperature = heat source. The fundamental heat equation.
2 Energy Balance 11 $Q^* - Q_H - Q_E \approx 0$ Net radiation must equal sensible + latent heat. Conservation of energy.
3 Directional Curvature 21 $\partial^2 T/\partial x^2 + \partial^2 T/\partial y^2$ Smoothness regularization in each spatial direction.
4 Anisotropy 21 $\|\partial^2 T/\partial x^2 - \partial^2 T/\partial y^2\|$ Penalizes isotropy where Stage 0 detected directional structure.
5 Gradient-Flux (Fourier) 26 $q + \kappa \nabla T \approx 0$ Heat flows down the temperature gradient. Fourier's law of conduction.
6 Gaussian Curvature 26 $\det(H_T)$ Penalizes extreme saddle points or spikes in predicted temperature field.
7 Alpha Smoothness 26 $\|\nabla \alpha(s)\|^2$ Treatment response α(s) should vary smoothly — physics doesn't jump.
8 Alpha Prior 26 $\|\alpha(s) - \alpha_\text{mix}\|^2$ Regularize α toward the ProcessRateNet mixture prior.
9 Transient varies $\partial T/\partial t$ matching Temporal gradient consistency for multi-snapshot data.
10 Nocturnal Cooling varies Night $\partial T/\partial t < 0$ At night, surfaces must cool (radiative cooling calibration).
Each term ramps linearly over 5 epochs after activation. This staged activation is the curriculum — described in the next section. Starting everything at once would cause training instability. The curriculum is what makes the optimization tractable.
Section 6

Training Curriculum — Teaching in Stages

You can't throw a neural network all 10 PDE constraints plus causal alignment plus surrogate fitting at once. The curriculum is a principled training schedule that adds complexity in waves, like learning to walk before running.

Stage A
Warmup
Physics weight = 0. Only data fit + surrogate alignment. Network first learns to predict.
Stage B
Physics Ramp
Heat diffusion → energy balance → directional terms activate sequentially. Each ramps linearly over 5 epochs.
Stage C
Joint Optimization
All loss terms active. Lambdas at CMA-ES optimized values. Full physics + data + JEPA alignment.
Stage D
SWA Averaging
Stochastic Weight Averaging over cyclic LR. Averages across a flat basin for better generalization.
Lambda Ramp Schedule
$$\lambda_k(t) = \lambda_k^\text{final} \cdot \text{clip}\!\left(\frac{t - t_k^\text{start}}{5}, 0, 1\right)$$

Term $k$ activates at epoch $t_k^\text{start}$ and ramps to its full weight over 5 epochs. This prevents gradient shock when new physics terms turn on.

CMA-ES Lambda Optimization
The optimizer that tunes the optimizer

The relative weights between data loss, PDE terms, surrogate alignment, and JEPA alignment are not hand-tuned. They are found by CMA-ES (Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy) — an evolutionary black-box optimizer that treats the entire training run as a function to minimize.

This is meta-optimization: optimizing the hyperparameters of the optimization process itself. CMA-ES adapts a full covariance matrix over the lambda space, so it discovers correlated tuning directions that grid search would miss entirely.

Section 7

Spatial Models — Four Surrogates + Differentiable Versions

Before the neural meta-learner, four classical spatial models run as base learners. Their out-of-fold predictions become inputs to the neural architecture. SPARC also maintains differentiable versions of each so gradients can flow through them.

GWR — Geographically Weighted Regression
Coefficients that vary across space

Ordinary regression assumes the relationship between predictors and outcome is constant everywhere. GWR relaxes this — each location has its own set of coefficients $\beta_k(s)$, estimated using a spatial kernel that down-weights distant observations.

GWR Model
$$\hat{y}(s) = \sum_k \beta_k(s) \cdot X_k + \beta_0(s)$$

$\beta_k(s)$ estimated by kernel-weighted WLS at each location. Bandwidth from KernelField.

Spatially-varying coefficients Physics priors per β Adaptive bandwidth
GWRF — Geographically Weighted Random Forest
Nonlinear local models

Random forests that are trained and evaluated with spatial kernel weighting. Each location effectively has its own model that pays more attention to nearby training observations. Handles nonlinear interactions that GWR cannot.

GWRF kernel weighting
$$\hat{y}(s) = \text{RF}\!\left(X; W(s)\right), \quad w_{ij} = \mathcal{K}\!\left(\frac{d(s_i, s_j)}{b}\right)$$

$b$ = bandwidth from KernelField. $\mathcal{K}$ = bisquare kernel. Auto-subsampled for N>10,000.

GGPGAM — Generalized GP-augmented Additive Model
Smooth spatial effects + nonlinear features

Combines spatial smoothing with flexible feature-response curves. Uses tensor-product splines for interaction effects between space and predictors.

GGPGAM structure
$$\hat{y}(s, X) = s(x,y) + \sum_k s(f_k) + \sum_k \text{te}(x, f_k)$$

$s(\cdot)$ = smooth spline, $\text{te}(\cdot)$ = tensor-product interaction. Elevation scale auto-detected.

Differentiable Surrogates
Gradients flow through the base models

For backpropagation to work through the CityHead, SPARC implements differentiable PyTorch versions of GWR (FiLM-modulated MLP + anisotropic warping), GWRF (FiLM + kernel-weighted blending), and GGPGAM (spatial + feature smoothers).

Laplacian Eigenmaps — Spatial Basis Functions
The geometry of space encoded as features

Build a KNN graph over spatial locations. Compute the normalized graph Laplacian. Its eigenvectors are smooth functions over the spatial domain — the $k$-th eigenvector represents the $k$-th lowest-frequency spatial pattern. SPARC uses these as effect modifiers in causal inference to capture spatial confounding.

Normalized Graph Laplacian
$$L = I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2}, \quad L \mathbf{v}_k = \lambda_k \mathbf{v}_k$$

Eigenvectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_k$ form a spatial basis. Used as effect modifiers in CATE estimation.

Section 8

JEPA — Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture

SPARC includes a spatial adaptation of Meta's I-JEPA architecture. Instead of predicting pixels, it predicts latent embeddings of neighboring spatial regions — learning compressed representations of spatial structure without reconstructing raw data.

Why JEPA instead of autoencoder?
Predict representations, not pixels

An autoencoder tries to reconstruct every pixel of a masked region — wasting capacity on irrelevant detail. JEPA instead predicts the latent embedding of the target region. This forces learning high-level, abstract spatial representations.

In SPARC's case, the "pixels" are spatial feature vectors. The JEPA pretraining gives the SharedTrunk a rich understanding of spatial structure before it ever sees a PDE loss term.

Action-Conditioned Prediction
What happens to this patch if we intervene?

The LatentPredictor is conditioned on an ActionEmbedding — an encoding of (treatment type, magnitude Δx, time offset Δt). This lets the predictor answer: "if we plant trees at location A, what does the latent representation of location B look like?"

FiLM Conditioning
$$\mathbf{h}_{i+1} = \text{LayerNorm}(\gamma_a \odot \mathbf{h}_i + \beta_a), \quad (\gamma_a, \beta_a) = W_a \cdot \mathbf{a}$$

FiLM = Feature-wise Linear Modulation. Zero-initialized → identity at start of training.

Section 9

Causal Inference Engine — Stage 3

Stage 3 is where SPARC separates association from causation. Four complementary methods triangulate causal effect estimates. This is not "correlation with a causal story" — it's formal causal identification under Bayesian uncertainty.

Why causal inference matters here: If trees correlate with lower temperatures, it could be because trees cause cooling — or because wealthy neighborhoods plant more trees and have more AC. SPARC's causal engine tries to distinguish these. It matters because interventions (planting trees) work through causal pathways, not correlational ones.
MC³ — Markov Chain Monte Carlo Model Comparison
Finding the most likely causal graph

Given data, which causal DAG (directed acyclic graph) is most plausible? MC³ runs Bayesian model selection using the BGe (Bayesian Gaussian Equivalent) marginal likelihood score. It uses parallel tempering — four MCMC chains at different "temperatures" — to avoid getting stuck in local optima.

BGe Score
$$P(\mathcal{G} | \text{data}) \propto P(\text{data} | \mathcal{G}) \cdot P(\mathcal{G})$$

Physics-informed prior: edges suggested by spatial autocorrelation get higher prior probability. Parallel tempering at $T \in \{1.0, 0.75, 0.55, 0.40\}$.

DAG structure learning BGe scoring Parallel tempering
NUTS — No U-Turn Sampler
Full Bayesian posteriors over causal coefficients

For each edge in the causal DAG, SPARC estimates the full posterior distribution over its coefficient — not just a point estimate. Uses numpyro's NUTS sampler with priors informed by the GWR β posteriors from Stage 2.

Bayesian GLM per edge
$$y_\text{resid} = \beta \cdot t_\text{resid} + \varepsilon, \quad \beta \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\text{GWR}, \sigma_\text{GWR}^2)$$

2000 samples, 800 warmup, target acceptance 0.90. Output: full posterior over each causal coefficient, not just a p-value.

numpyro GWR-informed prior 2000 posterior samples
DML — Double Machine Learning + Spatial CATE
Heterogeneous treatment effects across space

Double ML removes confounding via cross-fitted residualization. The outcome and treatment are separately regressed on confounders; the residuals are used to estimate the treatment effect. EconML's CausalForestDML then estimates how this effect varies by location.

DML Residualization
$$\tilde{Y} = Y - \hat{m}(X), \quad \tilde{T} = T - \hat{g}(X)$$ $$\tau(s) = \text{CausalForest}(\tilde{Y}/\tilde{T}; \text{covariates}(s))$$

Effect modifiers include Laplacian eigenmaps (spatial basis) and anisotropy-warped coordinates.

Causal PDP — Dose-Response Curves
How much tree cover is enough?

Not just "does treatment help" but "how much treatment is needed for how much benefit." Bayesian posterior samples give full uncertainty bands. Saturation detection finds the "knee" — the dose beyond which marginal benefit drops below 50% of peak.

Saturation Knee
$$t^* = \min\left\{t : \frac{\partial \tau(t)}{\partial t} < 0.5 \cdot \max_t \frac{\partial \tau}{\partial t}\right\}$$

Tells planners: "adding more than $t^*$ canopy cover gives diminishing returns." Actionable policy insight.

Section 10

Decision Engine — From Predictions to Actions

The final stage turns model outputs into a ranked, budget-constrained, equity-weighted list of interventions. This is where science becomes policy.

4-Tier Scenario Simulation
Nested counterfactuals
  • Tier 1 Baseline — No intervention, current trajectory
  • Tier 2 Physics — What physics says intervention does
  • Tier 3 Causal Direct — Causal model, direct effect only
  • Tier 4 DAG Indirect — Full DAG, all propagated effects

The four tiers quantify disagreement between physics, direct causal, and cascaded causal estimates — a built-in robustness check.

Equity Weighting
Not all locations are equal

Each candidate location gets an equity score combining population vulnerability, historical underinvestment, and climate exposure layers. Benefits at high-equity locations are up-weighted in the objective.

Ranking Score
$$\text{score}(c) = w_c \cdot \frac{\mu_c - \lambda \sigma_c}{k_c + \varepsilon}$$

$w_c$ = equity weight, $\mu_c$ = expected benefit, $\sigma_c$ = uncertainty, $k_c$ = cost. Risk-adjusted benefit per dollar per equity.

Causal Bandit
Learning allocation adaptively

A contextual bandit with Thompson sampling that treats intervention allocation as a sequential decision problem. Balances exploration (trying interventions we're less sure about) with exploitation (doubling down on what works).

Thompson sampling Spatial context Posterior update

As interventions are deployed and outcomes observed, the bandit updates its posterior and improves future allocations.

Treatment Cascades — Physics-Preserving Interventions
Changing canopy cover changes NDVI, albedo, surface moisture…

When a scenario says "increase Pct_Canopy by 10%", it's not just one feature that changes. Trees raise NDVI, lower albedo, increase latent heat flux, reduce surface runoff. SPARC encodes these covariate relationships as learned linear cascades so that all correlated feature changes propagate through the model — maintaining physical consistency.

Treatment Cascade
$$\Delta X_k = \sum_j C_{kj} \cdot \Delta T_j$$

$C_{kj}$ = learned cascade coefficient from treatment $j$ to feature $k$. Estimated from observational covariance with physical constraints. Preserves energy balance.
