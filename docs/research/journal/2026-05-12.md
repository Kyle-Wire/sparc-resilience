## Blue-Sky Ideas

### A. Optimal Transport for Cross-City Trunk Alignment
**Source field:** Optimal transport (Villani 2008; Peyré & Cuturi 2019)

**Core idea:** EWC penalizes *parameter* changes to prevent catastrophic forgetting. An alternative: penalize *distributional* divergence between trunk activation distributions across cities. Given cities A and B, the Wasserstein distance W₂(p_A, p_B) between their trunk activation distributions acts as a geometry-preserving alignment loss rather than a parameter-level penalty. When training on city B, the loss includes `λ_OT * W₂(trunk_activations_B, coreset_activations_A)` instead of the EWC quadratic penalty. This preserves the latent geometry (not just parameter magnitude) across cities, which should produce better-calibrated uncertainty on unseen cities.

**Relevant SPARC modules:** `sparc/training/ewc.py` (EWC penalizer, would be replaced/augmented), `sparc/training/replay.py` (coreset already stores 400-point representative activations), `sparc/registry/city_registry.py` (manages coresets across cities).

**Potential payoff:** More principled cross-city generalization; trunk latent space is geometrically consistent rather than parameterically frozen; compatible with JEPA (both operate on latent representations). The coreset's 400 points are already selected by K-medoids to be representative — they are a natural empirical distribution for Wasserstein comparison.

**Implementation path:** Python Optimal Transport library (`POT`), or `geomloss` for scalable Sinkhorn. Add `wasserstein_trunk_alignment()` in `sparc/training/ewc.py` as an alternative penalty.

### B. Sheaf Laplacian for Multi-Scale MAUP-Resistant Consistency
**Source field:** Sheaf theory / topological signal processing (Hansen & Ghrist 2020; Bodnar et al. 2022)

**Core idea:** SPARC computes a cardinal-neighbor Laplacian for the PDE physics loss. A *cellular sheaf* generalises this: instead of scalar values at each location, each node and edge carries a vector stalk, and the sheaf Laplacian enforces *consistency conditions* between multi-scale representations. For SPARC: if the model produces predictions at 100m, 500m, and census-block resolution, a sheaf over the spatial graph encodes the condition that predictions at finer scales must aggregate consistently to coarser scales. This is the formal structure underlying MAUP-resistance. The sheaf Coboundary operator becomes an additional loss term that penalizes scale-inconsistent predictions — not just local smoothness.

**Relevant SPARC modules:** `sparc/physics/pde_operators.py` (Laplacian already computed), `_build_cardinal_neighbors()` in `v2_neural_training.py` (the base spatial graph), `sparc/causal/spatial_cate.py` (multi-resolution CATE could be sheaf-constrained).

**Potential payoff:** Formal MAUP guarantee (a unique SPARC differentiator); publishable methodology; naturally extends to the `maup_sensitivity_analysis()` stub in the roadmap. The `sheaf_restriction_loss()` would be a new physics term alongside the existing 10-term PDE curriculum.

## Implementation Note — PDE-informed MC³ prior edge probabilities (2026-05-13)
- Files changed: `sparc/causal/mc3.py`, `tests/test_v2_modules.py`
- What was done: Added `pde_residual_edge_probs(data, node_names, coords, knn_k=8, alpha_base=0.1) -> np.ndarray`. For each ordered node pair (i,j) fits the bivariate ANM in both directions (i→j and j→i) via OLS, then scores each direction by the **spatial Laplacian energy** of its residuals — the mean squared deviation of residuals from their k-NN spatial mean. Lower energy = more spatially homogeneous residuals = more PDE-consistent direction. Relative energies are converted to edge probabilities via sigmoid: `p(i→j) = σ(L(j→i residuals) − L(i→j residuals))`, then blended with `alpha_base` uniform prior. Added `PhysicsInformedGraphPrior.from_pde_residuals()` classmethod. Extended `MC3Results` with `physical_plausibility_score: dict[str,float]`. Extended `run_mc3()` with optional `pde_coords` and `pde_knn_k` params — when supplied, computes per-winning-DAG-edge plausibility (fraction of Laplacian energy explained by the data-favoured direction) and stores in `physical_plausibility_score`. Added `TestPDEMC3` (5 synthetic-data tests): shape/range checks, direction asymmetry, classmethod, `physical_plausibility_score` population, and the backlog success criterion (mis-specified DAG gets lower log_prior).
- Tests: 5 `TestPDEMC3` + 1 `TestMC3::test_dag_structure_roundtrip` + 15 causal_gaps/discovery = 21 passed.
- What was learned: Spatial Laplacian energy is a natural proxy for PDE residual quality without requiring the full PDE forward pass — it measures whether residuals are spatially independent (PDE-consistent) vs. spatially structured (confounder leakage). The sigmoid conversion ensures the probability matrix is always in (alpha_base, 1−alpha_base) and both directions of an undirected pair sum to ~1.
