### Root Causes Identified

**1. Head covariate shift (CONFIRMED)**  
CityHead is being fine-tuned from a Raleigh trunk checkpoint. Raleigh training distribution: ~84°F mean.
Philadelphia target distribution: ~95°F mean. The `regression_head.bias` is anchored at the Raleigh scale.
With `weight_decay=1e-4` (global default), the bias doesn't move enough. `build_optimizer()` applies the
same weight decay to trunk AND city head — the trunk is also being regularized when only the head needs it.

Evidence: Predictions "off by ~11°F" (Raleigh→Philly shift) consistent across windows. Morning window
R² improves more than midday → different Raleigh/Philly distributions by time-of-day amplify the shift.

**2. Stochastic convergence (CONFIRMED)**  
No seed set before `SPARCMetaLearner` construction in `_exec_cv_fold`. Head weight init is
`torch.nn.init.xavier_uniform_` (default for `nn.Linear`) — draws from `Uniform(-sqrt(3/fan), sqrt(3/fan))`.
Different runs start from different head biases → converge to different local minima. Across train16–22,
runs are non-comparable because the seed was not fixed.

**Gap:** `build_optimizer()` in `sparc/training/optimizer.py` applies one `weight_decay` to all groups.

**Roadmap ref:** Phase 2 (Transfer Learning Quality — CityHead fine-tuning best practices)

## Blue-Sky Ideas

### Idea 1 — Temporal Covariate Shift Correction via Distributional Alignment
**Source field:** Domain adaptation — Maximum Mean Discrepancy (Gretton et al. 2012), CORAL (Sun & Saenko 2016)
**SPARC connection:** The "never simultaneously positive" window problem suggests the CityHead learns a
single bias that represents the mean of all training windows. If Raleigh morning/midday distributions
differ from Philly morning/midday differently, a single scalar bias can't compensate both.
**Extension:** Before CityHead fine-tuning, compute per-window CORAL loss:
`L_CORAL = ‖Cov(Raleigh_window) - Cov(Philly_window)‖_F²`. Minimize this during head adaptation.
**Potential payoff:** Could solve the "never simultaneously positive" problem entirely by aligning
per-window feature covariance before the head is adapted.
**Infrastructure:** `sparc/training/ewc.py` already has Wasserstein alignment; CORAL is simpler (~10 lines).

### Idea 2 — EMA Momentum Schedule from JEPA Literature (V-JEPA 2 insight)
**Source field:** V-JEPA 2 (Bardes et al. 2025) — reports that cosine-scheduled EMA momentum
(τ: 0.996→1.0 over training) outperforms fixed τ by enabling rapid early adaptation and stable late-phase
representations. SPARC's `EMATrunk` uses a fixed `ema_momentum` from config.
**SPARC connection:** `sparc/training/jepa_loss.py::EMATrunk` — add cosine schedule to `tau` parameter.
**Potential payoff:** Better JEPA trunk geometry → better trunk transfer → lower CityHead covariate shift (reinforces T-1).
**Infrastructure:** `EMATrunk` EMA update call is at a known location; schedule is ~5 lines.
